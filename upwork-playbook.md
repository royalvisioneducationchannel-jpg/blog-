# Upwork Playbook – Muhammad Saleem

Rules to follow for every job search, proposal and client message.
Skills: WordPress, Elementor, WooCommerce, Meta (Facebook/Instagram) Ads, Meta Pixel, Photoshop, Illustrator. No Canva jobs.

## 1. Hard rules (never break)
- Claude only advises: never applies, submits, accepts or sends anything without explicit approval.
- No off-platform contact in proposals or messages: no WhatsApp, phone, email or links to them (Upwork blocks it).
- Never ask for client passwords. Use partner / admin access only.
- Be honest: no invented numbers, experience, reviews or screenshots.
- No auto-bidding or third-party bidding tools (risk to the account).
- Never start monthly work on an unfunded milestone. Ask the client to fund it first (they still release after review).

## 2. Finding jobs
- Apply within the first 15–60 minutes after posting. Watch "Most Recent" feed, best time 8 PM – 2 AM PKT (US clients active).
- Filters: payment verified, few proposals (under 10–15 ideal), client has spend history and good rating (check reviews freelancers gave the client).
- Prefer "Less than 1 week" duration jobs for quick wins and fast reviews.
- Use keyword combos and minus words, e.g. `Elementor fix -Canva`, `WordPress form fix`, `Meta ads Pakistan`, `Facebook pixel`, `WooCommerce fix`.
- Save the best searches and check them daily.
- Search Pakistan-specific jobs (local market, Urdu, PKR clients) – less competition.
- Read the skills list but judge by the actual task (skills lists are often generic).
- If someone is already "interviewing", apply fast or skip.

## 3. Bidding and Connects
- Bid near or slightly below the average bid; never far above the budget. Don't go too cheap on good clients.
- Early stage: 10–20% below market rate, raise after reviews.
- Boost only cheaply and only for strong jobs. Save Connects.
- Rate-increase fields: leave empty, or "Every 12 months" + 5%.
- Duration: pick the shortest honest option (e.g. "Less than 1 week" for small fixes).

## 4. Writing proposals
- Open with the client's problem and the solution in line 1 – not "I am a skilled…".
- Give a short step-by-step plan (e.g. backup → fix → test → report).
- Show real, measurable results only (e.g. real Meta ads data: 765 conversations, PKR 75 per conversation on the best campaign).
- Offer a free mini-audit (point out 2–3 issues) instead of free full work.
- End with 2–3 specific questions (site link, what exactly is broken, which pages).
- Follow any required keyword the client asks for (e.g. "Malabis").
- Attach the relevant portfolio PDF.
- Optional standout: a short silent Loom video with captions showing the issue/plan.
- Keep it short, specific, and use the client's name if known.

## 5. Messaging clients
- One clear, complete message. Never send "?" or repeated pings.
- Use the client's name (not "mam"/"sir").
- Reply fast (within 15 minutes when possible) – keep notifications on.
- Follow up once politely after a reasonable wait, not more.
- Budget changes, pauses or reallocations only with the client's written approval on Upwork.
- Ads go live only after the client approves previews.

## 6. Profile
- Title: study ~25 ideal jobs, use their most common keywords. Example: "WordPress & Elementor Expert | Meta Ads Specialist".
- Overview: results and case studies, not just a skills list.
- Professional photo (no selfie).
- Request real testimonials from past clients (Profile → Request a testimonial), including off-Upwork clients.
- Upload only real certifications.
- Focus on a niche; portfolio shows only the best, most relevant work.
- Consider an Upwork Project Catalog listing (fixed-price packages).

## 7. After winning
- Deliver fast, send a short report with screenshots of tests.
- Always ask for a review and long-term work (maintenance, monthly ads).

## 8. Extra proposal tricks (from "32 Upwork Proposal Hacks", John Morris)
- Find the client's name in past reviews on the job page if it's not in the post; use it in line 1.
- Mirror the client's tone: formal client → formal reply, casual client → casual reply.
- Answer the hiring criteria the client repeats most (e.g. "fast", "mobile", "today").
- Optional: a short (<1 min) intro video link in addition to the written proposal (YouTube unlisted / Loom), not instead of it.
- Upwork cover letters are plain text: no bold/italic. Use CAPS headings, short lines and bullets.
- One polite follow-up after a few days if no reply; never more.
- "Out-niche" bigger freelancers: be the specialist (Elementor fixes, Meta Pixel, WooCommerce) rather than a generalist.
- Promise only what you can deliver; meet deadlines and send updates.
